Last time I said most of the Wii stuff I mastered during the rollout was shovelware. I was expecting this to be one of those. It is not.

I played Wiiware already, with La-Mulana, but let's talk about it specifically. Wiiware was the name for downloadable games off the Wii shop channel, with its sick-ass tunes, and were almost never sold directly in stores. It was like the Xbox Live Arcade for Wii stuff. And much like Xbox360 and PS3 VS the Wii for regularly released games, a lot of it is shovelware motion slop. Just shit for grandma or little kids that uses a waggle motion... right?

No, not at all. This is Art of Balance, and it really surprised me.

![Game](https://imgur.com/i5XTzHm.png){: .center-block :}

Art of Balance is very, very simple. You have a little zen bowl of water, and a bunch of blocks. You need to balance those blocks in such a way that it doesn't ripple the water. Usually the way the water ripples is shit falling into it, but dipping stuff in is off limits, too. And... that's it! There are four worlds. 124 levels in total, I think. Just balance stuff.

![Game](https://imgur.com/Ut5Gcxh.png){: .center-block :}

There is a LITTLE more than that. There are glass pieces which can break if you put too many pieces on them, as well as pieces that will break if you put ANYTHING on them after enough time has passed. Sometimes you WANT things to break, and can plan accordingly.

There's no one answers for any of these puzzles. It's a physics puzzle, and physics in videogames are fucked up and wonky, always. So you can find some truly diabolical ways to get through these levels.

I actually had a blast playing this game. It probably cost, what, 15 dollars on release? Easily worth it. It uses the motion controls for precise careful placement, has a chill atmosphere, and is really rewarding to finally beat that messed up level you've been stuck on. I can see why this is a bit of a cult classic for Wiiware.
